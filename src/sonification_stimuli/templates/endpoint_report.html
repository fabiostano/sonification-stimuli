Which base is most selective?

Proposed score = target benefit − λ × unintended-change penalty. Higher is better under this formula. Loudness is always penalized; missing estimates never count as zero.

Green bars show target benefit, red bars show the weighted penalty; the score is their difference. Full-protocol scores are unavailable if any required estimate is missing. Zero means no net benefit, not ideal quality. The common basis can exclude controls and must be interpreted within its stated scope.

Formula, normalization and per-control scores

For each proxy j, sⱼ is the maximum observed value across this cohort. This shared scale avoids division by a near-zero reference. Target benefit T = (high − low)/(2s_target), so reversed endpoint ordering reduces the reward. Coupling C is the weighted RMS of both endpoint changes from reference in unrelated proxies, divided by their shared scales; opposite changes cannot cancel. Loudness has weight 1. Pulse frequency and depth share one domain weight when both are included. Overall scores average the per-control scores with the same domain balancing. Centroid, RMS and crest factor remain available below but are excluded from this index to avoid counting extra proxies of the same attributes.

Scales are fitted to this dataset; adding bases can change scores and rankings. They are saved in base-selectivity.json. These are acoustic/model proxies, not validated perceptual quality or mental-state scores. λ expresses a chosen trade-off, not a fitted scientific constant.

† The reference lies outside the ordered level-1 to level-5 endpoints; inspect this manipulation. Inactive controls can have zero benefit and zero penalty, but do not demonstrate successful manipulation.
